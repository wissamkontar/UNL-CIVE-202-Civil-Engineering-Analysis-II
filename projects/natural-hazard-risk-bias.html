Project 5H

Natural Hazard Risk Analysis

Students use FEMA NRI and SVI data to compare natural hazard risk across multiple states and examine how risk relates to population impacts.

Category Infrastructure Analysis
Focus Skill Integrate Data Analysis Skills
Tools Python, Pandas, GeoPandas, Visualization
01

Client Prompt

Client Prompt

Risk Averse, LLC was pleased with the earlier natural hazard risk analysis and has asked your team to continue investigating hazard risk in the United States. This time, the client wants to focus on one specific natural hazard across six different states.

Students will use FEMA National Risk Index data, and may also use Social Vulnerability Index data, to analyze how risk differs across states and how that risk relates to the population potentially impacted. The analysis should help the client understand regional patterns, risk differences, and the implications for communities exposed to natural hazards.

Client Requests

  1. Select one natural hazard to analyze across six states, ideally states that are geographically close enough to support regional conclusions.
  2. Use Census Tract-level FEMA National Risk Index data for the selected hazard and states.
  3. Define risk using either the NRI method or a clearly documented alternative method based on available NRI and SVI variables.
  4. Create a summary plot showing how the chosen hazard risk differs across the selected states.
  5. Analyze how natural hazard risk relates to population using a table, plot, or other appropriate visual representation.
  6. Interpret whether regional trends appear in the data and explain why those trends may exist.

Deliverables and Requirements

Deliverables

  • Cleaned and organized NRI and supporting data files used in the Python analysis.
  • A Python notebook or code file documenting the full risk analysis workflow.
  • Python functions used to clean, filter, join, summarize, or visualize the data.
  • At least two appropriate graphs, maps, or summary tables that support the client’s hazard risk questions.
  • A summary plot comparing the chosen hazard risk across six states.
  • A table or plot showing how natural hazard risk relates to population.
  • A scope of work describing the analysis tasks completed for the client.
  • A Gantt chart showing project tasks, responsibilities, dates, and task bars.
  • A timesheet documenting hours spent on each major project activity.
  • Technical and non-technical executive summaries.
  • A written report summarizing findings and implications for communities affected by natural hazards.

Report Requirements

  • A clear explanation of the selected natural hazard, selected states, and rationale for the regional comparison.
  • A methods section explaining how NRI and any SVI or population data were selected, cleaned, filtered, joined, and analyzed.
  • A description of how risk was defined, including justification and references if the team uses an alternative risk definition.
  • A discussion of how the risk of the selected natural hazard differs across the six states, supported by generated figures and tables.
  • A discussion of whether regional trends were observed, supported by research or contextual evidence.
  • A discussion of how the natural hazard has impacted communities, especially in relation to population and/or SVI values.
  • References and citations for all datasets, documentation, and supporting research used in the report.
