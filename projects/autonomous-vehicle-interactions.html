Project 5E

Autonomous Vehicle Interaction Analysis

Students analyze trajectory data to compare autonomous vehicles, passenger vehicles, and pedestrians near an intersection.

Category Transportation Engineering
Focus Skill Integrate Data Analysis Skills
Tools Python, Pandas, Visualization, Trajectory Data
01

Client Prompt

Client Prompt

As transportation systems begin to include autonomous vehicles operating alongside traditional vehicles and pedestrians, engineers need to understand how different agents behave near intersections. The U.S. Department of Transportation has asked your team to analyze a subset of the Third Generation Simulation dataset from the Foggy Bottom segment.

Students will use 200 seconds of trajectory data to evaluate whether autonomous vehicles appear safer or more predictable than other agents. Rather than comparing full trajectories, students should focus on localized interactions, speed patterns, acceleration or deceleration behavior, and movement near intersection zones.

Client Requests

  1. Compare the behavior of different transportation agents, such as autonomous vehicles, passenger vehicles, and pedestrians.
  2. Analyze localized behavior patterns near intersections or pedestrian areas.
  3. Compare acceleration, deceleration, speed profiles, average speeds, or other measurable behavior patterns.
  4. Present findings using clear summary tables and visualizations.
  5. Create a visual representation of agent movement at the intersection, either as a static plot or an animation.
  6. Interpret whether the available data supports or challenges the claim that autonomous vehicles are safer or more predictable.

Deliverables and Requirements

Deliverables

  • A cleaned and organized trajectory dataset used in the Python analysis.
  • A Python notebook or code file documenting the full analysis workflow.
  • Python functions used to filter, compare, or summarize agent behavior.
  • At least two appropriate graphs, tables, or movement visualizations that support the client’s questions.
  • A static plot or animation representing agent movement at the intersection.
  • A scope of work describing the analysis tasks completed for the client.
  • A Gantt chart showing project tasks, responsibilities, dates, and task bars.
  • A timesheet documenting hours spent on each major project activity.
  • Technical and non-technical executive summaries.
  • A written report summarizing findings and transportation engineering insights.

Report Requirements

  • A dataset description explaining the available information, data structure, key variables, and any preprocessing steps.
  • A methods section explaining how the team selected agents, locations, behavior metrics, and visualization approaches.
  • A quantitative comparison of autonomous vehicle behavior relative to other transportation agents.
  • Clear figures, plots, tables, or animations that support the interpretation.
  • A discussion of whether autonomous vehicles appear safer or more predictable based on the limited 200-second dataset.
  • References and citations for all datasets, documentation, and supporting research used in the report.
